Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) Assessment

About Glasgow Coma Scale

The Glasgow Coma Scale is a neurological scale used to assess conscious level after a head injury. It evaluates three aspects of responsiveness: eye opening, verbal response, and motor response. Scores range from 3 (worst) to 15 (best).

Eye Opening Response (E)

Eyes open spontaneously without stimulation
Eyes open in response to speech or shouting
Eyes open only in response to physical stimulation
No eye opening response to any stimulus

Verbal Response (V)

Coherent, oriented to time, place, and person
Coherent speech but disoriented and confused
Intelligible speech with inappropriate or random words
Moaning, groaning, or incomprehensible sounds
No vocal response

Motor Response (M)

Follows simple motor commands correctly
Moves limb to remove painful stimulus
Flexion withdrawal from painful stimulus
Decorticate posturing (arms flex, legs extend)
Decerebrate posturing (arms and legs extend)
No motor response to stimulation

    Important Notes:

    • Always assess the best response from the patient
    • Record individual component scores (e.g., E4V5M6 = GCS 15)
    • Consider factors that may affect assessment (intubation, sedation, language barriers)
    • Reassess regularly and document trends over time
    • GCS ≤8 generally indicates need for airway protection
