# Chapter 14: Gynaecological Conditions

## 14.1 Gynaecological Conditions

### 14.1.1 Dysmenorrhoea

ICD-10 CODE: N94.6

Primary dysmenorrhoea occurs more commonly among adolescents and young women. Symptoms usually begin 6 to 12 months after menarche and occur mainly with ovulatory cycles.

Generally, severity of symptoms decreases with age, sexual activity, and childbirth.

**Clinical features**

Clinical features include:

- Lower abdominal cramping
- Backache
- Headache
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhoea
- Fainting
- Fever
- Fatigue
- Dizziness

**Differential diagnosis**

Differential diagnosis includes:

- Endometriosis
- Other causes of lower abdominal pain

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| **Non-pharmacological treatment**<br><br>- Encourage the patient to rest or sleep.<br>- Encourage the patient to do some exercises.<br>- Advise the patient to apply a warm compress to the abdomen.<br>- Encourage the patient to wear loose-fitting clothes.<br>- Advise the patient to have a diet low in fats and supplements such as magnesium, vitamin B1, vitamin E, and zinc. | HC2 |
| **Pharmacological treatment**<br><br>- Give NSAIDs, for example ibuprofen 200 to 400 mg every 8 hours as required.<br>- Other medicines include paracetamol 1 g every 6 hours in case of mild pain, or diclofenac 50 mg every 8 hours for severe forms. | HC2/HC4 |
| **Review and referral**<br><br>- Review the patient after 5 days.<br>- If there is no response or if symptoms are recurrent, refer for specialist management.<br>- In secondary dysmenorrhoea, treat the cause, for example PID, with antibiotics. | HC4 |

### 14.1.2 Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)

ICD-10 CODE: N70-N73

**Risk factors**

Risk factors include:

- Previous pelvic inflammatory disease
- Presence of bacterial vaginosis
- Multiple or new sexual partners
- History of STIs in the patient or her partner
- History of abortion
- Young age, especially below 25 years
- Postpartum endometritis

**Causes**

Pelvic inflammatory disease is often due to multiple pathogens, including:

- *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*
- *Chlamydia trachomatis*
- *Mycoplasma*
- *Gardnerella*
- *Bacteroides*
- Gram-negative bacilli, for example *Escherichia coli*

**Clinical features**

**Clinical features**

Clinical features include:

- Lower abdominal pain, usually less than 2 weeks, plus one or more of the following:
  - Dysuria
  - Fever
  - Vaginal discharge, which may be smelly and mixed with pus
  - Painful sexual intercourse, also called dyspareunia
  - Cervical motion tenderness, where vaginal examination produces tenderness when the cervix is moved
  - Abnormal uterine bleeding

In severe PID:

- Swellings may be felt if there is pus in the tubes or pelvic abscess.
- There may be signs of peritonitis, such as rebound tenderness.

**Complications of PID**

Complications include:

- Infertility
- Ectopic pregnancy
- Chronic pelvic pain

> **Warning**
> Do not treat chronic pelvic pain with antibiotics.
>
**Differential diagnosis**

Differential diagnosis includes:

- Ectopic pregnancy
- Threatened abortion
- Ovulation pain
- Acute appendicitis
- Complicated or twisted ovarian cyst
- Cancer of the cervix

**Investigations**

Investigations include:

- Speculum examination
- Pregnancy test
- Pus swab for culture and sensitivity
- Ultrasound, if available, for detection of tubo-ovarian masses, free fluid, and peritonitis

For pus swab collection, the speculum protects the sampling item. The sample may be from the endocervix, aspirate from the endometrial cavity or curettings, or an aspirate through the posterior pouch.

**Management**

Treatment is based on a combination of medicines that cover the multiple microorganisms involved.

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| **Outpatient treatment**<br><br>- Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM stat.<br><br>Or, if ceftriaxone is not available:<br><br>- Cefixime 400 mg stat.<br><br>Plus:<br><br>- Doxycycline 100 mg orally every 12 hours for 14 days.<br>- Metronidazole 400 mg orally twice daily for 14 days.<br>- Treat sexual partners as for urethral discharge syndrome to avoid reinfection. | HC3 |
| **Treatment in pregnancy**<br><br>- Use erythromycin 500 mg every 6 hours for 14 days instead of doxycycline. | HC3 |
| **If severe or not improving after 7 days**<br><br>- Refer for ultrasound scan and parenteral treatment.<br>- Give ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily plus metronidazole 500 mg IV every 8 hours until clinical improvement.<br>- Then continue oral regimen as above. | HC4 |
| **Notes**<br><br>- All women with PID should be tested for HIV.<br>- Abstain from sex or use barrier methods during the course of treatment.<br>- Do not take alcohol when taking metronidazole.<br>- Avoid sex during menstruation and for 6 weeks after an abortion.<br>- In IUD users with PID, the IUD need not be removed. However, if there is no clinical improvement within 48 to 72 hours of initiating treatment, providers should consider removing the IUD and help the patient choose an alternative contraceptive method. See Chapter 15. | HC3 |

### 14.1.3 Abnormal Uterine Bleeding

ICD-10 CODE: N39.9

Abnormal uterine bleeding is any vaginal bleeding that represents a variation from the normal pattern of regular menstruation.

**Causes**

Causes include:

- Hormonal abnormalities, including ovulatory dysfunction
- Abortion
- Ectopic pregnancy
- Uterine diseases such as fibroids and polyps
- Cancers, including cervical, uterine, and rarely vaginal cancer
- Infections, including STIs
- Coagulation disorders
- Iatrogenic causes, including IUDs and hormonal contraceptives

**Clinical features**

Clinical features include:

- Abnormal menstrual pattern
- Continuous or subcontinuous bleeding
- Acute and heavy bleeding
- Light and subcontinuous bleeding

**Investigations**

Investigations include:

- Pregnancy test to exclude abortion and pregnancy
- Haemoglobin level
- Vaginal examination for cervical and vaginal abnormalities, for example cervical cancer
- Abdominal ultrasound

**Management**

Management is based on the possible cause.

| Cause or issue | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| General measures | Ferrous sulphate or Fefol 1 tablet once or twice daily | HC2 |
| Positive pregnancy test | See section on abortion and ectopic pregnancy in Chapter 16 | HC4 |
| Bleeding in postmenopausal woman | Refer for specialist assessment because of possible endometrial pathology | RR |
| Lesion, ulcer, or growth in vagina or on cervix | Refer for specialist assessment | RR |
| Suspected fibroid, such as bulky hard uterus | Use analgesics, give iron supplement, and refer for ultrasound scan | H |
| Other signs of infection | Treat as PID and review | HC3 |
| Women on family planning | See sections on family planning methods and side effects in Chapter 15 | HC2 |

### 14.1.4 Menopause

ICD-10 CODE: Z78.0

Menopause is the cessation of menstruation in a female and usually occurs spontaneously at the age of 45 to 55 years.

Perimenopause is the time around menopause and can last a few years until menopause has set in.

Menopause can also be caused by surgical removal of the ovaries.

**Clinical features**

Clinical features include:

- Hot flushes, meaning sudden unanticipated unpleasant waves of body heat, ranging from mild to intense
- Night sweats
- Palpitations
- Headaches
- Insomnia
- Tiredness
- Irregular menstruation until cessation
- Vaginal atrophy and dryness
- Loss of libido
- Painful intercourse
- Bladder irritability
- Incontinence
- UTIs
- Weight gain, sometimes
- Skin changes, including dryness, thinning, loss of head hair, and increase or loss of body hair
- Mood swings and emotional changes such as depression, irritability, short temper, and weepiness
- Lack of concentration
- Failing memory
- Osteoporosis
- Denture problems

**Investigations**

- Exclude pregnancy.

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| **Non-pharmacological treatment**<br><br>- Explain the process of menopause to the patient and reassure her that it is normal.<br>- Suggest lifestyle adjustment. | HC2 |
| **Lifestyle measures**<br><br>- Follow a healthy diet.<br>- Sleep and exercise enough.<br>- Wear loose, light clothing.<br>- Avoid alcohol.<br>- Eat a diet low in fats and high in fruits and vegetables.<br>- Take food-rich supplements such as magnesium, vitamin B1, vitamin E, and zinc.<br>- Take calcium-rich foods or supplements such as milk and soya beans, and vitamin D supplements. | HC2 |
| **Screening and symptom control**<br><br>- Screen for cardiovascular disease, including hypertension and heart disease.<br>- Screen for urinary incontinence.<br>- For severe symptoms such as severe hot flushes or depression, consider fluoxetine 20 mg daily. | HC4 |
| **Referral**<br><br>Urgently refer any menopausal woman with vaginal bleeding for further assessment. | RR |
