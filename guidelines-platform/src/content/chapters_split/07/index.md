# Chapter 7: Renal and Urinary Diseases

## 7.1 Renal Diseases

### 7.1.1 Acute Renal Failure

**ICD-10 CODE:** N17

Acute renal failure is acute impairment of renal function.

**Causes**

- Compromised renal perfusion, such as dehydration, heart failure, or shock
- Obstructed urinary flow
- Damage to renal tissue by infectious and inflammatory diseases, such as glomerulonephritis
- Intoxications
- Nephrotoxic medicines

**Clinical features**

- Oliguria, defined as urine flow below 1 mL/kg/hour
- Generalised oedema
- Hypertension
- Heart failure
- Dyspnoea
- Nausea and vomiting
- Anorexia
- Lethargy
- Convulsions

**Differential diagnosis**

- Other renal disorders
- Biventricular heart failure

**Investigations**

- Urine analysis for blood, protein, leucocytes, and casts
- Urea, creatinine, and electrolytes

**Management**

Management of acute kidney conditions can be started at hospital level, but the patient should be referred to a higher level for more appropriate management.

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Treat underlying conditions, such as dehydration. | HC4 |
| Monitor fluid input and output. | HC4 |
| Calculate daily fluid requirements as 10 mL/kg plus total losses through urine, vomitus, and diarrhoea. | HC4 |
| Monitor blood pressure twice daily. | HC4 |
| Weigh the patient daily. | HC4 |
| Restrict salt intake to less than 2 g daily, or about half a teaspoonful daily. | HC4 |
| Restrict potassium intake, including oranges, bananas, vegetables, meat, and fizzy drinks. | HC4 |
| Give moderate protein intake. | HC4 |
| Ensure adequate calories in the diet. | HC4 |
| Check urine and electrolytes frequently. | HC4 |
| Treat complications, such as infections, hypertension, and convulsions, adjusting medicine doses according to the clinical response where appropriate. | RR |
| If there is oliguria, give IV furosemide according to response. High doses may be necessary. | RR |
| If there is no response to the above general measures, worsening kidney function, or anuria, refer for specialist management including possible dialysis as soon as possible and before the patient’s condition becomes critical. | HC4 |

> **Caution**
> Do not give medicines that may worsen kidney damage.
>
> Use gentamicin with caution.
>
### 7.1.2 Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

**ICD-10 CODE:** N18

Chronic kidney disease is chronic impairment of kidney function.

**Causes and risk factors**

- Diabetes mellitus
- Hypertension or cardiovascular disease
- Age above 50 years
- Kidney stones
- Medicines, especially pain killers such as diclofenac, ibuprofen, and other NSAIDs
- Family history of kidney disease
- HIV/AIDS

**Clinical features**

Most patients with CKD have no symptoms until the disease is advanced.

Patients may present with:

- Features of the predisposing risk factor
- Anaemia
- Lethargy
- Easy fatigue
- Loss of appetite
- Nausea and vomiting
- Skin itching
- Bone pains
- Body swelling
- Loin pain

**Differential diagnosis**

- Other causes of chronic anaemia
- Heart failure
- Protein-energy malnutrition
- Chronic liver disease

**Investigations**

- Creatinine, urea, and electrolytes
- Urine dipstick for protein and blood
- Kidney ultrasound
- Urine dipstick for protein and blood, and blood pressure measurement at least once a year in high-risk patients
- In diabetic patients, urine microalbumin where possible, or spot urine protein:creatinine ratio at least once a year
- If abnormalities are detected, perform serum creatinine and calculate GFR

**Refer the following patients for specialist attention**

- Children
- Persistent proteinuria or haematuria beyond 3 months
- GFR below 60 mL/min or creatinine above 1.9 mg/dL
- Familial kidney disease, such as polycystic kidney disease

**How to screen for CKD in patients at risk**

Screen high-risk patients using:

- Blood pressure measurement
- Urine dipstick for protein and blood
- Serum creatinine and estimated GFR where indicated
- Urine microalbumin or protein:creatinine ratio in diabetic patients where possible

**Management**

Treatment of end-stage renal disease is complex and expensive, and is available only at national referral hospital level.

**Goals**

- Establish diagnosis and treat reversible disease
- Identify co-morbid conditions and manage complications of CKD
- Slow progression of CKD by optimising treatment
- Plan renal replacement therapy before end-stage kidney disease is reached

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Preserve kidney function in patients with CKD. | HC4 |
| Encourage lifestyle modification, including weight loss, stopping smoking, exercise, healthy balanced diet, lipid control, and salt restriction. | HC4 |
| Control blood pressure. Target blood pressure is 130/80 mmHg, or lower in children. | HC4 |
| Use ACE inhibitors as first-line antihypertensives for diabetic patients and patients with proteinuria, together with a low-salt diet. | HC4 |
| In diabetic patients, blood pressure control is paramount. | HC4 |
| Optimise blood sugar control. Target HbA1c is below 7%. | HC4 |
| Reduce proteinuria using ACE inhibitors and/or ARBs. Target proteinuria is below 1 g/day. | RR |
| Avoid nephrotoxic medicines, such as NSAIDs, celecoxibs, aminoglycosides, and contrast agents. | HR |
| Prevent complications. For anaemia, consider iron and folic acid supplements. Target haemoglobin is 11 to 12 g/dL. | HC4 |
| For bone mineral disease, consider adding calcium. | HC4 |
| For fluid retention or oliguria, give furosemide tablets according to response. High doses may be necessary. | HC4 |
| Refer for dialysis in end-stage cases. | RR |

> **Caution**
> Start ACE inhibitors at low doses and monitor renal function.
>
**Prevention**

- Screen high-risk patients.
- Optimise treatment of risk factors.
- Use treatments that slow progression in the initial phases.
- Avoid nephrotoxic medicines.

### 7.1.3 Use of Medicines in Renal Failure

Be very careful when prescribing any medicine in renal failure or renal impairment. Check available prescribing information, such as the Practical Guidelines for Dispensing 2015, before prescribing.

Many medicines are excreted through the kidneys and accumulate when urinary output is reduced.

Some medicines are presented as sodium or potassium salts and may contribute to accumulation of these electrolytes.

With life-threatening infections, such as meningitis, use normal or high doses of antibiotics initially, then reduce doses once the condition has responded.

**Medicines usually safe in renal failure**

- Doxycycline
- Erythromycin
- Benzylpenicillin, maximum 6 g daily in severe impairment
- Phenytoin
- Rifampicin

**Medicines to use with care in reduced doses**

- ACE inhibitors, such as captopril
- Amoxicillin
- Chloramphenicol, avoid in severe impairment
- Ciprofloxacin
- Cotrimoxazole
- Diazepam
- Digoxin
- Insulin
- Isoniazid-containing medicines
- Pethidine, increase dose interval and avoid in severe impairment
- Phenobarbital
- Propranolol

**Medicines to avoid**

- Acetylsalicylic acid and other NSAIDs, such as ibuprofen and indomethacin
- Codeine
- Ethambutol
- Gentamicin
- Metformin
- Nalidixic acid
- Nitrofurantoin
- Streptomycin
- Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF)


### 7.1.4 Glomerulonephritis

**ICD-10 CODE:** N00–N01

Glomerulonephritis is inflammatory kidney disease. It commonly follows immune reactions after infection, usually 1 to 5 weeks after a streptococcal skin or throat infection.

**Clinical features**

- Common in children above 3 years and adolescents
- Haematuria, presenting as red or tea-coloured urine
- Oedema, especially puffiness of the face or around the eyes
- Less commonly, generalised body swelling
- Discomfort in the kidney area, including abdominal or back pain
- Anorexia
- General weakness or malaise
- High blood pressure for age
- Headache
- Visual disturbances
- Vomiting
- Occasionally pulmonary oedema with dyspnoea
- Convulsions in hypertensive crisis
- Oliguria as renal failure sets in
- Evidence of primary streptococcal infection, usually acute tonsillitis with cervical adenitis
- Less commonly, evidence of skin sepsis

**Differential diagnosis**

- Kidney infections, such as TB or pyelonephritis
- Kidney tumours
- Heart failure
- Malnutrition
- Allergic reactions

**Investigations**

- Urine analysis for protein
- Urine microscopy for red blood cells, casts, and white blood cells
- Blood urea and creatinine
- Antistreptolysin O titre (ASOT)
- Electrolytes
- Kidney ultrasound

**Management**

Inflammatory kidney disease with oedema, hypertension, and oliguria should be referred to a regional hospital for specialised management.

| Clinical situation | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| General management | Monitor urine output, blood pressure, and daily weight. | H |
| General management | Restrict fluid input if there is oliguria. | H |
| General management | Restrict salt and regulate protein in the diet if there is oliguria. | H |
| General management | Avoid, or use with caution, medicines excreted by the kidney. See section 7.1.3. | H |
| General management | Treat any continuing hypertension. See section 4.1.6. | H |
| Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis | Treat the primary streptococcal infection with a 10-day course of phenoxymethylpenicillin 500 mg every 6 hours. | H |
| Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis | Children: phenoxymethylpenicillin 10 to 20 mg/kg per dose. | H |
| Alternative treatment | Give amoxicillin 500 mg every 8 hours for 10 days. | H |
| Alternative treatment | Children: amoxicillin 20 mg/kg per dose. | H |
| Penicillin allergy | Give erythromycin 500 mg every 6 hours for 10 days. | H |
| Penicillin allergy | Children: erythromycin 15 mg/kg per dose. | H |
| Fluid overload or oedema | Give furosemide 80 mg IV as a slow bolus. | H |
| Fluid overload or oedema | Children: furosemide 1 mg/kg every 8 to 12 hours. | H |
| High blood pressure | Give nifedipine 20 mg every 12 hours. | H |
| High blood pressure in children | Refer to a specialist. | H |

**Prevention**

- Treat throat and skin infections promptly and effectively.
- Avoid overcrowding.
- Ensure adequate ventilation in dwellings.


### 7.1.5 Nephrotic Syndrome

**ICD-10 CODE:** N04

Nephrotic syndrome is a disorder characterised by loss of protein in the urine due to damage of the kidney. It is common in children.

**Causes**

- Idiopathic or unknown cause, which accounts for the majority of cases
- Congenital nephrotic syndrome, which is rare
- Secondary causes, including:
    - Post-streptococcal acute glomerulonephritis
    - Malaria
    - Allergy
    - Urinary tract infection
    - Hepatitis B
    - HIV

**Clinical features**

- Generalised oedema
- Severe loss of protein in urine, or proteinuria
- Low serum albumin levels, or hypoalbuminaemia
- Hyperlipidaemia, or high blood cholesterol

**Investigations**

- Investigations as for acute glomerulonephritis
- 24-hour urine protein quantification, or albumin creatinine ratio (ACR)
- Serum protein
- Serum cholesterol

**Differential diagnosis**

- Cardiac failure
- Liver disease
- Malnutrition with oedema, such as kwashiorkor
- Malabsorption syndrome
- Allergic states causing generalised body swelling
- Chronic glomerulonephritis

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Restrict salt intake to less than 2 g daily, equivalent to less than half a teaspoon per day. | H |
| Restrict water or fluid intake. | H |
| Moderate both salt and water/fluid intake until diuresis is induced and swelling is subsiding. This can take several weeks. | H |
| Give furosemide 40 to 80 mg each morning to induce diuresis. | H |
| Children: give furosemide 1 to 2 mg/kg per dose. See notes below. | H |
| Give prednisolone 2 mg/kg daily, maximum 60 mg. | RR |
| Continue prednisolone until there is no further proteinuria, usually around 6 weeks. | RR |
| Gradually reduce the prednisolone dose after the first 4 weeks, for example by 0.5 mg/kg per day each week. | RR |
| When oedema has subsided, if the patient is still hypertensive, give appropriate treatment. See section 4.1.6. | RR |
| If there are clinical signs of suspected streptococcal infection, give antibiotics as for acute glomerulonephritis. | RR |
| If the patient is from an area endemic for schistosomiasis, give praziquantel 40 mg/kg as a single dose. | RR |
| If there is no improvement after 4 weeks, or if the patient relapses, refer for further management. | RR |


## 7.2 Urological Diseases

### 7.2.1 Acute Cystitis

**ICD-10 CODE:** N30

Acute cystitis is an infection or inflammation involving the bladder, which is part of the lower urinary tract. It is a common manifestation of uncomplicated urinary tract infection (UTI) in non-pregnant women.

Uncomplicated cystitis is less common in men and should be differentiated from prostatitis and urethritis, including sexually transmitted urethritis.

**Cause**

- Bacterial infection, usually gram-negative organisms from intestinal flora, such as *Escherichia coli*

**Clinical features**

- Dysuria, or pain and difficulty in passing urine
- Urgency of passing urine
- Frequent passing of small amounts of urine
- Suprapubic pain and tenderness
- Pyuria or haematuria, causing cloudy urine
- Foul-smelling urine
- Retention of urine in severe infection

**Investigations**

- Midstream urine analysis for:
    - Protein
    - Blood
    - Leucocytes
    - Nitrites
    - Sediment
- Urine culture and sensitivity if there are resistant or repeated infections

**Diagnostic criteria**

- Symptoms with or without leucocytes and/or nitrites on urine analysis

**Differential diagnosis**

- In women: vaginitis
- In men:
    - Urethritis, especially in young sexually active patients
    - Prostatitis, especially in older men, which may present with fever, chills, malaise, perineal pain, or confusion

> **Note**
> Asymptomatic bacteriuria or pyuria, meaning leucocytes in urine, does not need treatment except in risk groups such as pregnant women, patients undergoing urological interventions, and post-kidney transplant patients.
>
**Management**

| Clinical situation | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| Uncomplicated UTI or cystitis in non-pregnant women | Ensure high fluid intake. | HC2 |
| First-line treatment | Give nitrofurantoin 100 mg every 6 hours for 5 to 7 days. Advise the patient to take it after meals. | HC2 |
| First-line treatment in children | Give nitrofurantoin 3 mg/kg/day in divided doses every 6 hours for 7 days. | HC2 |
| Second-line treatment in adults | Give ciprofloxacin 500 mg every 12 hours for 7 days. | HC2 |
| Second-line treatment in children | Give amoxicillin 125 to 250 mg every 8 hours for 7 days. | HC2 |
| Poor response or recurrent infections | Refer for urine culture and sensitivity, further investigation, and further management. | HC2 |

> **Note**
> For urinary tract infection in pregnancy, see section 16.2.6.
>
**Prevention**

- Improve personal and genital hygiene.
- Pass urine after coitus.
- Drink plenty of fluids.

### 7.2.2 Acute Pyelonephritis

**ICD-10 CODE:** N10

Acute pyelonephritis is an upper urinary tract infection involving one or both kidneys, but usually not involving the glomeruli.

**Causes and risk factors**

- Bacterial infection, such as *Escherichia coli*, usually due to ascending infection through faecal, perineal, and urethral progression of bacteria
- Bladder outlet obstruction
- Malformations of the urinary tract
- Pregnancy
- HIV
- Old age
- Diabetes

**Clinical features**

- Loin pain
- Tenderness in one or both kidney areas, or renal angle tenderness
- Fever
- Rigors, or generalised body tremors
- Vomiting
- If associated with cystitis:
    - Dysuria
    - Urgency
    - Frequency
- Diarrhoea and convulsions, especially in children
- In infants and elderly patients, presentation may be limited to fever and poor feeding or disorientation, without other signs

**Differential diagnosis**

- Appendicitis
- Infection of the fallopian tubes, or salpingitis
- Infection of the gall bladder, or cholecystitis

**Investigations**

- Urine microscopy for pus cells and organisms
- Midstream urine culture and sensitivity
- Blood full count
- Blood culture and sensitivity
- Urea and electrolytes
- Ultrasound of kidneys and prostate

> **Note**
> The urine specimen should reach the laboratory within 2 hours of collection, or be refrigerated at 4°C for not more than 24 hours.
>
**Management**

| Clinical situation | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| General management | Ensure adequate fluid intake, orally or IV, to irrigate the bladder and dilute bacterial concentrations. | HC3 |
| Pain and fever | Give paracetamol 1 g every 6 to 8 hours. | HC3 |
| Outpatient treatment in adults only | Give ciprofloxacin 500 mg every 12 hours for 10 to 14 days. | HC3 |
| Severe cases, all children, or no response after 48 hours | Give ceftriaxone 1 g IV once daily. | HC3 |
| Severe cases in children | Give ceftriaxone 50 to 80 mg/kg IV once daily. | HC3 |
| Following initial response to parenteral therapy in adults | Consider changing to ciprofloxacin 750 mg every 12 hours to complete 10 days of treatment. | HC3 |
| Following initial response to parenteral therapy | Alternatively, give cefixime 200 mg every 12 hours to complete 10 days of treatment. | HC3 |
| Following initial response to parenteral therapy in children | Give cefixime 16 mg/kg on the first day, then 8 mg/kg to complete 10 days of treatment. | HC3 |
| Alternative regimen | Give gentamicin 5 to 7 mg/kg IV as one dose or in divided doses, with or without ampicillin 2 g IV every 6 hours. | HC3 |
| Alternative regimen in children | Give gentamicin 2.5 mg/kg every 8 hours, or 7.5 mg/kg once daily on outpatient basis, with or without ampicillin 25 mg/kg every 6 hours. | HC3 |
| No response after 72 hours | Consider referral. | HC3 |
| Children with recurrent infections | Refer to exclude urinary tract malformations. | HC2 |

**Prevention**

- Ensure perianal hygiene.
- Ensure regular complete emptying of the bladder.
- Encourage double voiding, meaning an additional attempt to empty the bladder after the initial urine flow ceases.

### 7.2.3 Prostatitis

**ICD-10 CODE:** N41

Prostatitis is acute inflammation or infection of the prostate, a gland present in males and located below the bladder around the proximal urethra.

**Cause**

- Bacterial infection, similar to urinary tract infection

**Clinical features**

- Fever
- Chills
- Rectal pain
- Perineal pain
- Low back pain
- Urinary urgency
- Frequency
- Dysuria
- Acute urinary retention
- Tender enlarged prostate on rectal examination

> **Caution**
> Avoid vigorous rectal examination.
>
**Investigations**

- Haemogram
- Urine analysis
- Urine culture and sensitivity

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Give IV fluids. | HC4 |
| Give antipyretics. | HC4 |
| Advise bed rest. | HC4 |
| Give stool softeners. | HC4 |
| Give ciprofloxacin 500 mg every 12 hours for 4 to 6 weeks. | HC4 |

### 7.2.4 Renal Colic

**ICD-10 CODE:** N23

Renal colic is acute severe pain in the loin, or kidney area, caused by obstruction of the ureters by a stone.

**Causes**

- Urinary stones
- Rarely, blood clot
- Rarely, tumour

**Clinical features**

- Acute severe colicky loin pain
- Pain may radiate to the iliac fossa, testes, or labia on the same side
- Dysuria may occur
- Nausea
- Vomiting

**Differential diagnosis**

- Lower urinary tract infection
- Acute upper urinary tract infection
- Other causes of acute abdominal pain

**Investigations**

- Urinalysis for blood
- Plain abdominal X-ray for radio-opaque stones
- Ultrasound

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Give oral or IV fluids to maintain hydration. | HC4 |
| Give antiemetics if necessary, such as metoclopramide 10 mg IM or IV. | HC4 |
| Give diclofenac 75 mg IM as a single dose. | HC4 |
| Give pethidine 50 to 100 mg IM as a single dose, if required. | HC4 |
| Refer if episodes are repeated or unresolved. | HC4 |

**Prevention**

- Ensure oral fluid intake of 3 to 4 L/day.
- Reduce salt intake.
- Reduce animal protein intake.

### 7.2.5 Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia

**ICD-10 CODE:** N40

Benign prostatic hyperplasia is enlargement of the prostate causing urinary symptoms. It is common in men above 50 years.

**Cause**

- Benign age-related growth of the prostate

**Clinical features**

Obstructive symptoms include:

- Weak urine stream
- Straining during micturition
- Hesitancy
- Intermittency
- Sensation of incomplete bladder emptying

Irritative symptoms include:

- Frequent micturition, especially at night
- Urgency
- Urge incontinence

Complications include:

- Acute urinary retention
- Frequent infections, which may precipitate symptoms

**Investigations**

- Urine analysis for blood and leucocytes
- Renal function tests
- Abdominal ultrasound

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Treat with antibiotics if infection is present. See prostatitis or acute cystitis in sections 7.2.3 and 7.2.1. | HC2 |
| Refer for surgical management if symptoms are severe. | RR |

### 7.2.6 Bladder Outlet Obstruction

Bladder outlet obstruction is obstruction of the urinary tract anywhere below the bladder, causing distension and incomplete emptying of the bladder. It may be acute, presenting as acute urinary retention, or chronic.

**Causes**

- Benign prostatic hyperplasia
- Prostate cancer
- Bladder tumours
- Bladder stones
- Pelvic masses
- Rarely, pregnancy
- Rarely, neurological causes
- Infection, which can precipitate acute retention

Chronic obstruction can cause hydronephrosis and chronic kidney damage.

**Clinical features**

Acute obstruction may present with:

- Painful and tender pelvic mass
- Difficulty passing urine

Chronic obstruction may present with:

- Obstructive and irritative urinary symptoms, as described under benign prostatic hyperplasia
- Painless pelvic mass

**Investigations**

- Urine analysis
- Urine culture and sensitivity
- Abdominal ultrasound
- Renal function tests
- Specialised investigations, such as cystourethrogram

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Insert a urethral catheter to relieve obstruction, usually size 18 F. | HC4 |
| Insert a suprapubic catheter if urethral catheterisation fails. | RR |
| Treat infection if present. | HC4 |
| Refer to a specialist for assessment and care. | RR |

### 7.2.7 Urinary Incontinence

**ICD-10 CODE:** N39.3–N39.4

Urinary incontinence is involuntary urine leakage.

**Causes and clinical features**

- Pelvic floor muscle dysfunction, such as following pregnancy, causing stress incontinence during straining, coughing, or sneezing
- Overactive bladder causing urge incontinence, with a sudden compelling need to urinate that is difficult to defer
- Anatomical problems causing continuous incontinence, such as vesicovaginal fistula or ectopic ureter
- Chronic bladder outlet obstruction causing overflow incontinence

**Investigations**

- Careful history and examination
- Urine analysis
- Abdominal ultrasound

**Management**

| Clinical situation | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| Stress incontinence | Advise pelvic floor exercises. | HC2 |
| Other causes of urinary incontinence | Manage according to the specific cause. | H |
