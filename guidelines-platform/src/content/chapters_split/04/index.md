# Chapter 4: Cardiovascular Diseases

## 4.1 Cardiovascular Conditions

### 4.1.1 Deep Vein Thrombosis / Pulmonary Embolism (DVT/PE)

**ICD-10 CODE:** I82.409

Deep vein thrombosis is clot formation within the deep venous system, usually in the calf, thigh, or pelvic veins. The clot may cause a local problem at the site of formation or may dislodge and lead to thromboembolism in other parts of the body, particularly the lungs, resulting in pulmonary embolism.

**Causes**

- Venous stasis, meaning slowing of blood flow
- Increased coagulability states
- Endothelial injury

**Risk factors**

- Immobilisation, prolonged bed rest, surgery, or limb paralysis
- Heart failure or myocardial infarction
- Blunt trauma or venous injury, including cannulation
- Oral contraceptive pill use
- Pregnancy and postpartum period
- Malignancies and some forms of chemotherapy
- Long-distance air travel
- Inherited thrombophilic states

**Clinical features**

Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism may be clinically silent in some patients.

Features of deep vein thrombosis may include:

- Pain, swelling, and warmth of the calf, thigh, or groin

Features of pulmonary embolism may include:

- Dyspnoea
- Tachycardia
- Chest pain
- Hypotension

Half of pulmonary embolism cases may be associated with silent DVT.

**Differential diagnosis**

For DVT:

- Cellulitis
- Myositis
- Phlebitis
- Contusion

For pulmonary embolism:

- Bronchopneumonia
- Myocardial infarction
- Other causes of dyspnoea and chest pain

**Investigations**

- Compression ultrasound with or without Doppler
- In suspected pulmonary embolism, chest CT angiogram
- Other useful but non-specific tests:
    - Blood D-dimer
    - ECG
    - Chest X-ray
    - Echocardiogram

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Give enoxaparin, a low molecular weight heparin, 1 mg/kg every 12 hours for at least 5 days. No routine monitoring is required. | H |
| Start warfarin 5 mg as a single evening dose on the same day as heparin. Continue overlap treatment with heparin and warfarin. Stop heparin when warfarin achieves an optimal INR of 2–3. | H |
| Continue warfarin maintenance dose as a single daily dose, adjusted according to INR. Typical maintenance dose may range from 2.5 mg to 7.5 mg daily. Some patients may require higher maintenance doses, especially where interacting medicines are present. | H |
| If enoxaparin is not available, give unfractionated heparin 5,000 units IV bolus, then 1,000 units hourly, or 17,500 units subcutaneously every 12 hours for 5 days. Adjust dose according to activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT). | H |
| Alternative unfractionated heparin regimen: give 333 units/kg subcutaneously as an initial dose, followed by 250 units/kg subcutaneously every 12 hours. Use with warfarin as above. | H |

**Alternative to warfarin and heparin combination**

Rivaroxaban may be used as an alternative anticoagulant.

- No need to initiate with heparin.
- Give rivaroxaban 15 mg orally twice daily for 3 weeks, then 20 mg once daily for the duration of anticoagulation.
- Patients should take the medicine at the same time every day to prevent re-thrombosis.
- Do not use in pregnant or breastfeeding mothers.
- It is associated with reduced bleeding risk compared with warfarin.

Apixaban may also be used as an alternative anticoagulant.

- Give apixaban 10 mg twice daily for 7 days, then 5 mg twice daily for the duration of anticoagulation.
- It is associated with reduced bleeding risk compared with warfarin.
- Do not use in pregnant or breastfeeding mothers.

**Notes**

- Monitor for bleeding complications.
- When using apixaban, patients should be encouraged to ambulate as soon as possible.
- See section 1.3.10 for treatment of warfarin overdose and the PGD 2015 monograph on protamine for excessive heparin dose.
- Do not start therapy with warfarin alone because it initially increases the risk of thrombus progression.
- Encourage early mobilisation.
- Provide prophylaxis with enoxaparin 40 mg subcutaneously once daily in acutely ill medical patients and in prolonged admission.

### 4.1.2 Infective Endocarditis

**ICD-10 CODE:** I33.0

Infective endocarditis is an infection of the heart valves and the lining of the heart chambers by microorganisms. It is usually bacterial and rarely fungal.

**Causes**

Infective endocarditis is classified into three main types:

- **Sub-acute endocarditis:** usually caused by low-virulence organisms such as *Streptococcus viridans*.
- **Acute endocarditis:** usually caused by common pyogenic organisms such as *Staphylococcus aureus*.
- **Post-operative endocarditis:** occurs after cardiac surgery or prosthetic heart valve placement. The most common organism involved is *Staphylococcus aureus*.

**Clinical features**

The disease may present as acute or chronic depending on the microorganism involved and the patient’s condition.

Common clinical features include:

- Fatigue and weight loss
- Low-grade fever and chills, or acute severe septicaemia
- Embolic phenomena affecting different organs, including the brain
- Heart failure
- Prominent or changing heart murmurs
- Splenomegaly or hepatomegaly
- Anaemia
- Splinter haemorrhages in the nail bed or retina
- Finger clubbing

The diagnostic triad is:

- Persistent fever
- Emboli
- Changing murmur

**Risk factors**

- Rheumatic heart disease
- Congenital heart disease
- Prosthetic valve
- Invasive dental, diagnostic, or surgical procedures, including cardiac catheterisation
- Immunosuppression
- Intravenous drug use

> **Note**
> Any unexplained fever in a patient with a heart valve problem should be regarded as possible infective endocarditis.
>
**Differential diagnosis**

- Cardiac failure with heart murmurs
- Febrile conditions associated with anaemia

**Investigations**

- Blood cultures. These are usually positive, and all efforts should be made to identify the responsible pathogen and obtain sensitivity data.
- At least three sets of blood cultures, 8 mL each, should be obtained from separate venepuncture sites at least one hour apart.
- Complete blood count
- ESR
- Urinalysis for microscopic haematuria and proteinuria
- Echocardiography
- ECG

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Bed rest. | H |
| Treat complications, such as heart failure. | H |
| Start initial empirical antibiotic therapy with benzylpenicillin 5 MU IV every 6 hours for 4 weeks. | H |
| Children: benzylpenicillin 50,000 IU/kg IV every 6 hours for 4 weeks. | H |
| Add gentamicin 1 mg/kg IV every 8 hours for 2 weeks. | RR |
| If staphylococcal infection is suspected, especially with acute onset, add cloxacillin 3 g IV every 6 hours. | RR |
| Children: cloxacillin 50 mg/kg IV every 6 hours for 4 weeks. | RR |
| If MRSA is suspected, give vancomycin 500 mg IV every 6 hours. | RR |
| Children: vancomycin 10 mg/kg IV, infused over 1 hour, every 6 hours for 6 weeks. | RR |
| Once the pathogen has been identified, amend treatment according to culture and sensitivity results. | RR |

**Prevention**

For patients at risk, such as those with valvular defects, congenital heart disease, or prosthetic valves, give prophylaxis before dental procedures and tonsillectomy.

- Give amoxicillin 2 g as a single dose 1 hour before the procedure.
- For children, give amoxicillin 50 mg/kg as a single dose 1 hour before the procedure.

### 4.1.3 Heart Failure

**ICD-10 CODE:** I50

Heart failure is a clinical syndrome caused by inadequate cardiac output for the body’s needs despite adequate venous return.

For management purposes, heart failure may be classified as:

- Congestive or acute heart failure
- Chronic heart failure
- Acute pulmonary oedema. See section 4.1.4.

**Causes**

- Hypertension
- Valvular heart disease, such as rheumatic heart disease
- Myocardial infarction
- Myocarditis
- Prolonged rapid irregular heartbeat or arrhythmias
- Congenital heart disease
- Severe anaemia
- Thyroid disease

**Clinical features in infants and young children**

- Respiratory distress with rapid breathing
- Cyanosis
- Wheezing
- Subcostal, intercostal, or sternal recession
- Rapid pulse
- Gallop rhythm
- Excessive sweating
- Tender hepatomegaly
- Difficulty with feeding
- Cardiomegaly

**Clinical features in older children and adults**

- Palpitations
- Shortness of breath
- Exercise intolerance
- Fatigue
- Orthopnoea
- Exertional dyspnoea
- Wheezing
- Rapid pulse
- Gallop rhythm
- Raised jugular venous pressure
- Dependent oedema
- Enlarged tender liver
- Basal crepitations

**Differential diagnosis**

- Severe anaemia
- Severe acute malnutrition
- Nephrotic syndrome
- Cirrhosis
- Severe pneumonia
- Any severe illness in infants

**Investigations**

- Chest X-ray
- Haemogram, including assessment for anaemia
- ESR
- Urea and electrolytes
- Echocardiogram
- ECG

**Management of acute or congestive heart failure**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Bed rest with the head of the bed elevated. | HC4 |
| Prop up the patient in a sitting position. | HC4 |
| Reduce salt intake and limit fluid intake to 1–1.5 L/day. | HC4 |
| Give furosemide 20–40 mg orally or IV daily or every 12 hours, increasing as required to 80–160 mg according to response. | HC4 |
| Children: furosemide 1 mg/kg orally or IV daily or every 12 hours according to response. Maximum dose: 8 mg/kg/day. | HC4 |
| Start ACE inhibitors at a low dose. Give enalapril 2.5 mg once daily and increase gradually over 2 weeks to 10–20 mg, maximum 40 mg, if tolerated. Alternatively, give lisinopril 5 mg once daily and increase gradually over 2 weeks to 40 mg. | H |
| Children: enalapril 0.1–1 mg/kg/day in 1–2 divided doses. | H |
| Alternatively, give captopril 6.25–12.5 mg every 8–12 hours and increase over 2–4 weeks to a maximum of 150 mg/day in divided doses. | H |
| Children: captopril 0.1–0.3 mg/kg every 8–12 hours. | H |
| If available, and when the patient is stable, add carvedilol 3.125 mg every 12 hours and increase gradually every 2 weeks to a maximum of 25 mg every 12 hours. Alternatively, give bisoprolol 1.25 mg once daily and increase gradually to a maximum of 10 mg once daily. | H |
| Children: carvedilol 0.05 mg/kg every 12 hours and increase gradually to a maximum of 0.35 mg/kg every 12 hours. | H |

**Additional medicines**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Spironolactone 25–50 mg once daily. | H |
| Children: spironolactone initially 1.5–3 mg/kg/day in divided doses. | H |
| Digoxin 125–250 micrograms daily. | HC4 |
| Children: digoxin maintenance dose 15 micrograms/kg/day. | HC4 |

> **Caution**
> Use ACE inhibitors and beta blockers with caution if systolic blood pressure is less than 90 mmHg. Monitor renal function.
>
> Use digoxin with caution in elderly patients and in patients with renal disease.
>
**Prevention**

- Manage risk factors.
- Diagnose and treat the underlying cause early, such as hypertension.
- Support treatment adherence.

**Chronic heart failure**

Patients with chronic heart failure need continuous treatment to control symptoms and prevent disease progression and complications.

**Management of chronic heart failure**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Periodic monitoring of body weight, blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation. | HC2 |
| Salt and fluid restriction. | HC2 |
| Limit alcohol intake. | HC2 |
| Regular exercise within the limits of symptoms. | HC2 |
| Continue treatment with the medicines listed above, with doses progressively increased to achieve control. | HC4 |

### 4.1.4 Pulmonary Oedema

**ICD-10 CODE:** I50.21

Pulmonary oedema may be:

- Cardiogenic
- Due to severe fluid overload, such as renal failure or iatrogenic fluid overload
- Non-cardiogenic, such as severe pneumonia, altitude sickness, inhalation of toxic gases, or acute respiratory distress syndrome

**Clinical features**

- Severe dyspnoea
- Rapid breathing
- Breathlessness
- Tachycardia
- Wheezing
- Cough with frothy blood-stained sputum

**Differential diagnosis**

- Pneumonia
- Pleural effusion
- Foreign body
- Trauma, including pneumothorax or pulmonary contusion

**Investigations**

- Chest X-ray
- ECG
- Renal function tests
- Electrolytes
- Echocardiography

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Prop up the patient in a sitting position. | HC4 |
| Give high-concentration oxygen. Start with 5 L/min and aim for SpO₂ >95%. | HC4 |
| Give furosemide 40–80 mg IM or slow IV. Repeat as needed up to every 2 hours according to response. | HC4 |
| Children: give furosemide 0.5–1.5 mg/kg every 8–12 hours. Maximum dose: 6 mg/kg/day. | HC4 |
| Give glyceryl trinitrate 500 micrograms sublingually every 4–6 hours. | H |
| Give morphine 5–15 mg IM or 2–4 mg slow IV. | HC4 |
| Children: give morphine 0.1 mg/kg slow IV as a single dose. | HC4 |
| Repeat the above measures every 4–6 hours until there is improvement. | HC4 |
| Consider digoxin loading dose IV 250 micrograms, given 3–4 times in the first 24 hours, then maintenance dose of 125–250 micrograms daily. | H |
| Children: give digoxin 10 micrograms/kg per dose as above, then maintenance dose of 15 micrograms/kg/day. | H |

> **Caution**
> Do not give a digoxin loading dose if the patient has had digoxin within the past 14 days. Give maintenance dose only.
>
**Prevention**

- Diagnose and treat cardiac conditions early.
- Support compliance with treatment for chronic cardiac conditions.
- Avoid fluid overload.

### 4.1.5 Atrial Fibrillation

**ICD-10 CODE:** I48

Atrial fibrillation is a common cardiac arrhythmia characterised by an irregular pulse due to loss of regular atrial electrical activity. Its onset may be acute or chronic, and it may be symptomatic or asymptomatic.

**Risk factors**

- Heart disease, including:
    - Heart failure
    - Valvular heart disease
    - Ischaemic heart disease
- Thyroid disease, especially hyperthyroidism

**Clinical features**

- Irregular pulse in frequency and volume
- Heart rate may be normal or very high
- Acute onset, often with high heart rate, may present with:
    - Palpitations
    - Dizziness
    - Fainting
    - Chest pain
    - Shortness of breath
- Chronic atrial fibrillation with normal or near-normal heart rate may be asymptomatic and discovered during routine checks
- May precipitate heart failure or pulmonary oedema
- May cause embolic stroke if clots form in the heart and dislodge to the cerebral circulation

**Investigations**

- ECG

**Objectives of management**

- Control heart rate
- Restore normal rhythm where possible, under specialist care
- Prevent or treat complications
- Treat underlying conditions

**Management**

| Clinical situation | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| Acute onset, high heart rate, or patient in congestive heart failure and/or pulmonary oedema | Treat heart failure according to section 4.1.3. Use digoxin and/or carvedilol or bisoprolol to reduce heart rate. | HC4 |
| Acute onset with high heart rate but no signs of heart failure | Use atenolol 50 mg to control heart rate. | HC4 |
| Chronic atrial fibrillation with normal heart rate | Treat underlying conditions only. | H |
| Need for stroke prevention assessment | Refer to regional level to assess indication for anticoagulation with aspirin or warfarin to prevent stroke. | H |

### 4.1.6 Hypertension

**ICD-10 CODE:** I10

Hypertension is persistently high resting blood pressure, defined as blood pressure >140/90 mmHg on at least two measurements taken five minutes apart with the patient seated, and confirmed on at least 2 or 3 occasions one week apart.

**Classification of blood pressure**

| Category | SBP, mmHg |  | DBP, mmHg |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Normal | <120 | and | <80 |
| Pre-hypertension | 120–139 | or | 80–89 |
| Hypertension, stage 1 | 140–159 | or | 90–99 |
| Hypertension, stage 2 | ≥160 | or | ≥100 |

SBP = systolic blood pressure. DBP = diastolic blood pressure.

**Causes**

In most cases, the cause is not known. This is referred to as essential hypertension.

**Secondary hypertension may be associated with:**

- Kidney disease
- Endocrine disease
- Eclampsia or pre-eclampsia
- Medicines, including steroids and decongestants containing caffeine or pseudoephedrine

**Risk factors**

- Family history
- Race
- Obesity
- Physical inactivity
- Excessive salt intake
- Excessive alcohol intake
- Diabetes
- Dyslipidaemia

**Clinical features**

Most patients are asymptomatic and are diagnosed during routine examination or screening.

General symptoms may include:

- Headache
- Palpitations
- Dizziness

Hypertension may also present through complications affecting:

- Brain, such as stroke
- Heart, such as heart failure
- Kidney, such as renal failure
- Eyes, such as impaired vision

**Differential diagnosis**

- Anxiety

**Investigations**

Investigations are done to identify complications and possible secondary causes of hypertension.

- Urinalysis
- Blood sugar
- Plasma urea and electrolytes
- Chest X-ray
- ECG

**Management of hypertension**

Target blood pressure is below 140/90 mmHg.

For patients with high cardiovascular disease risk, such as diabetes, coronary heart disease, stroke, or chronic kidney disease, the target blood pressure is <130/80 mmHg.

**Hypertension, stage 1**

| Step | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | Lifestyle adjustment: do not add extra salt to cooked food, increase physical activity or exercise, reduce body weight, stop smoking, and decrease alcohol intake. | HC3 |
| Step 2 | If lifestyle measures fail within 3 months, initiate medicine therapy while continuing lifestyle changes. Give amlodipine 5 mg once daily. | HC3 |
| Follow-up | If blood pressure is not controlled after 1 month, treat as stage 2 hypertension. | HC3 |

**Hypertension, stage 2**

| Step | Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|---|
| Step 3 | Give amlodipine 5 mg once daily plus an angiotensin II receptor blocker, such as losartan 50 mg, valsartan 80 mg, or telmisartan 40 mg once daily. Instead of an ARB, an ACE inhibitor such as lisinopril 20 mg or enalapril 5 mg once daily may be used. | HC3 |
| Step 4 | If blood pressure remains ≥140/90 mmHg after 1 month, give amlodipine 10 mg once daily plus losartan 50 mg, valsartan 80 mg, or telmisartan 40 mg once daily. | HC3 |
| Step 5 | If blood pressure remains ≥140/90 mmHg after 1 month, give amlodipine 10 mg once daily plus losartan 100 mg, valsartan 160 mg, or telmisartan 80 mg once daily, plus a thiazide diuretic such as hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg or bendroflumethiazide 5 mg once in the morning. | HC3 |
| Step 6 | If blood pressure remains ≥140/90 mmHg after 1 month, continue amlodipine 10 mg once daily plus losartan 100 mg, valsartan 160 mg, or telmisartan 80 mg once daily, plus a thiazide diuretic such as hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg or bendroflumethiazide 5 mg once in the morning. | HC3 |
| Step 7 | If blood pressure remains ≥140/90 mmHg, refer for further management at a higher level of care. | HC3 |

**Provision for specific patients**

- Assess cardiovascular disease risk in all patients with hypertension.
- Patients with diabetes, coronary heart disease, stroke, or chronic kidney disease are considered to have high cardiovascular disease risk.
- In people with high cardiovascular disease risk, target blood pressure is <130/80 mmHg.
- Start a statin, such as atorvastatin 20–40 mg once daily or simvastatin 20–40 mg once daily, in people at high risk.
- Start aspirin 75 mg in people with a prior heart attack or ischaemic stroke.
- Start a beta blocker, such as atenolol 50 mg, bisoprolol 5 mg, or nebivolol 5 mg once daily, in people who had a heart attack within the past 3 years.
- In patients with chronic kidney disease, a combination of an ACE inhibitor or ARB with a calcium channel blocker or diuretic is recommended as initial therapy.
- For hypertension secondary to thyroid disease, consider adding propranolol 40 mg twice daily.

> **Caution**
> In pregnancy, do not use ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or diuretics. Methyldopa and calcium channel blockers are safe to use.
>
> Do not use both an ACE inhibitor and an ARB together because of increased risk of side effects.
>
**Choice of antihypertensive medicine**

Choice of medicine may depend on concomitant risk factors or other conditions.

| Risk factor / condition | Suitable medicine consideration |
|---|---|
| Heart failure | Consider medicines appropriate for heart failure management, including ACE inhibitor or ARB, selected beta blocker, diuretic, and aldosterone antagonist where indicated. |
| Post-myocardial infarction | Consider beta blocker, ACE inhibitor or ARB, statin, and aspirin where indicated. |
| Angina | Consider beta blocker or calcium channel blocker where appropriate. |
| Diabetes | Consider ACE inhibitor or ARB, especially where kidney protection is needed. |
| Mild or moderate kidney disease | Consider ACE inhibitor or ARB, with renal function monitoring. |
| Advanced chronic kidney disease | Refer or manage with specialist input. |
| Stroke | Control blood pressure and manage cardiovascular risk according to stroke guidance. |
| Beta blocker selection | Where beta blocker use is indicated in heart failure, carvedilol or bisoprolol are preferred. |

**Prevention**

- Regular physical exercise
- Reduced salt intake
- Healthy diet
- Smoking cessation
- Periodic blood pressure screening

#### 4.1.6.1 Hypertensive Emergencies and Urgency

**ICD-10 CODE:** I16.2

Hypertensive emergency is blood pressure >180/110 mmHg with symptoms and acute life-threatening complications.

Complications may include:

- Hypertensive encephalopathy, presenting with severe headache, confusion, seizures, or visual disturbances
- Acute angina or acute myocardial infarction
- Pulmonary oedema
- Acute kidney failure
- Acute aortic dissection
- Eclampsia or pre-eclampsia. See sections 16.3.7 and 16.3.8.

**Management of hypertensive emergency**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Admit the patient and give parenteral medicines. | HC4 |
| Aim to lower blood pressure over 24 hours. Do not lower blood pressure too rapidly unless absolutely necessary. | HC4 |
| Treat according to the presenting complication. | HC4 |
| In acute ischaemic stroke, do not lower blood pressure below 220/120 mmHg. | HC4 |
| In acute aortic dissection, lower blood pressure rapidly. | HC4 |
| In pulmonary oedema or acute myocardial infarction, treat the complication. | HC4 |
| Give IV furosemide 40–80 mg where indicated. | HC4 |
| If aggressive blood pressure lowering is needed, give IV hydralazine 5–10 mg slowly over 20 minutes. Check blood pressure regularly and repeat the dose after 20–30 minutes if necessary. | HC4 |

**Hypertensive urgency**

Hypertensive urgency is blood pressure >180/110 mmHg without evidence of target organ damage, such as pulmonary oedema, cardiac ischaemia, neurological deficits, or acute renal failure.

**Management of hypertensive urgency**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Admit the patient. | HC4 |
| Treat with a combination of oral antihypertensive therapy, such as ACE inhibitor or ARB plus calcium channel blocker, with or without a diuretic. | HC4 |
| Aim to lower blood pressure over the next 48–72 hours. | HC4 |

### 4.1.7 Ischaemic Heart Disease (Coronary Heart Disease)

**ICD-10 CODE:** I20, I21, I25

Ischaemic heart disease is a condition in which there is insufficient blood flow through the coronary arteries of the heart, leading to ischaemia and/or infarction.

**Cause**

- Deposition of fatty material, such as cholesterol plaques, and platelet aggregation inside the coronary arteries causing partial or total obstruction of blood flow

**Risk factors**

- Hypertension
- Diabetes mellitus
- Smoking
- Obesity
- Unhealthy diet
- Physical inactivity
- Hyperlipidaemia
- Family history of heart disease

**Clinical features**

Acute coronary syndrome, including acute myocardial infarction, may present with:

- Prolonged chest pain, which may be localised on the left or central part of the chest
- Pain ranging from mild to severe
- Pain sometimes radiating to the left arm, neck, and back
- Sweating
- Dyspnoea
- Vomiting
- Anxiety
- Low blood pressure
- Tachycardia

Stable angina may present with:

- Tightness in the chest or a sense of oppression
- Worsening on exertion
- Relief by rest
- Pain lasting only a few minutes

Sudden cardiac death may occur, usually due to fatal arrhythmias.

**Differential diagnosis**

- Indigestion
- Hiatus hernia
- Peptic ulcer
- Pleurisy
- Pericarditis
- Pulmonary embolism
- Dissecting aneurysm

**Investigations**

- Cardiac enzymes, including CPK and troponin
- ECG, including resting ECG and stress ECG
- Echocardiogram

**Management of acute coronary syndrome**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Give acetylsalicylic acid 300 mg as a single dose, to be chewed. | HC2 |
| Refer immediately to hospital. | HC2 |
| Give glyceryl trinitrate 500 micrograms sublingually. Repeat after 5 minutes if there is no response. | H |
| Give oxygen therapy if SpO₂ <94%. | H |
| Give morphine 2.5–5 mg IV if pain persists. | H |
| Give simvastatin 40 mg or atorvastatin 40 mg. | H |
| Give enoxaparin 1 mg/kg subcutaneously every 12 hours. | H |
| Treat complications accordingly, such as pulmonary oedema or arrhythmias. | H |

**Consider adding**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Beta blocker if there are no contraindications, such as systolic BP <90 mmHg or heart rate <60 bpm. Examples include atenolol 25–50 mg daily. | H |
| Ensure close observation of pulse rate and circulatory status. | H |
| ACE inhibitor, such as enalapril 2.5–10 mg daily. | H |
| Refer for further management at a higher level of care if unstable. | H |

When the patient is stable, continue:

- Acetylsalicylic acid 75 mg once daily
- Atorvastatin 40 mg daily
- Beta blocker, such as atenolol, carvedilol, or bisoprolol, and ACE inhibitor if tolerated
- Lifestyle changes, including healthy diet, no smoking, regular exercise, and control of other risk factors

**Management of stable angina**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Aggressive control of risk factors, including hypertension, diabetes, smoking, and obesity. | HC2 |
| Give acetylsalicylic acid 75–150 mg once daily. | H |
| Give atorvastatin 40 mg once daily. | H |
| Give beta blockers, such as atenolol 25–100 mg, if the patient is not diabetic. | H |
| Refer to a higher level of care if still uncontrolled. | H |

**Prevention**

- Low-fat, low-cholesterol diet
- Stop smoking
- Effective control of hypertension and diabetes mellitus
- Consider treatment with acetylsalicylic acid and statin in patients with multiple risk factors

### 4.1.8 Pericarditis

**ICD-10 CODE:** I30

Pericarditis is inflammation of the heart membrane, the pericardium.

It may be:

- Acute and self-limiting
- Subacute or chronic
- Fibrinous
- Serous
- Haemorrhagic
- Purulent

**Causes**

- Idiopathic or viral causes, which are the most common causes, such as Coxsackie A and B, influenza A and B, and varicella
- Bacterial causes, such as mycobacterium, staphylococcus, meningococcus, streptococcus, pneumococcus, gonococcus, and mycoplasma
- Fungal causes, such as histoplasmosis
- Severe kidney failure
- Hypersensitivity, such as acute rheumatic fever
- Myocardial infarction
- Radiation
- Trauma
- Neoplasms

**Clinical features**

Pericarditis without effusion may present with:

- Retrosternal pain radiating to the shoulder
- Pain that worsens on deep breathing, movement, change of position, or exercise
- Pericardial rub, which is a diagnostic sign

Pericardial effusion may present with:

- Reduced cardiac impulses
- Muffled heart sounds
- Cardiomegaly

Cardiac tamponade may occur in cases of massive effusion or constrictive pericarditis and may present with:

- Dyspnoea
- Restlessness
- Rising pulmonary and systemic venous pressure
- Rapid heart rate
- Pulsus paradoxus
- Low blood pressure
- Low-output cardiac failure

**Differential diagnosis**

- Other causes of chest pain
- Other causes of heart failure

**Investigations**

- ECG
- Chest X-ray
- Echocardiography

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| If viral or idiopathic, advise rest. | H |
| Give ibuprofen 600 mg every 8 hours. | H |
| If there is fluid, perform pericardial tapping. | H |
| If there are other causes, treat accordingly. | H |

**Prevention**

- Early detection and treatment of potential treatable causes

### 4.1.9 Rheumatic Fever

**ICD-10 CODE:** I00, I01

Rheumatic fever is a systemic connective tissue disease that follows a streptococcal upper respiratory tract infection. It may involve the heart, joints, skin, subcutaneous tissue, and central nervous system.

The first attack usually occurs between 3 and 15 years of age.

**Cause**

- Hypersensitivity reaction to group A streptococcal throat infection

**Clinical features**

- Arthritis, typically migrating asymmetric polyarthritis
- Acute rheumatic carditis, with signs of cardiac failure, murmurs, and pericarditis
- Subcutaneous nodules
- Chorea, presenting as involuntary movements of the limbs
- Skin rash
- Other minor signs and symptoms, including fever, arthralgia, and abnormal laboratory findings

**Differential diagnosis**

- Any form of arthralgia or arthritis, including sickle cell disease and haemophilia
- Pyrexia with cardiac failure

**Investigations**

- Haemogram, including assessment for raised ESR
- Chest X-ray
- ECG
- Echocardiography
- Antistreptolysin O titre (ASOT)

**Diagnostic criteria: revised Jones criteria**

Diagnosis requires evidence of recent streptococcal infection, such as:

- Elevated ASO titre or other streptococcal antibody titres
- Positive throat swab for group A beta-haemolytic streptococcus

Plus either:

- Two major manifestations, or
- One major manifestation and two minor manifestations

| Major manifestations | Minor manifestations |
|---|---|
| Polyarthritis | Polyarthralgia |
| Carditis | Fever |

**Duration of prophylaxis**

Duration depends on disease severity:

- Rheumatic fever without carditis: for 5 years or until age 18 to 21 years
- Carditis without residual heart disease: for 10 years or until age 25 years
- Carditis with residual heart disease: until age 40 to 45 years or for life

**Prevention**

- Early diagnosis and treatment of group A streptococcal throat infection
- Avoid overcrowding
- Improve housing conditions
- Promote good nutrition

### 4.1.10 Rheumatic Heart Disease

**ICD-10 CODE:** I05–I09

Rheumatic heart disease is disease of the heart valves following an episode of rheumatic fever.

The valves commonly involved are:

- Mitral valve, leading to stenosis, incompetence, or both
- Aortic valve, leading to stenosis, incompetence, or both

**Clinical features**

- Heart failure
- Arrhythmias and palpitations
- Thromboembolic complications, such as stroke
- Heart murmurs depending on the valve affected and the nature of the lesion
- Asymptomatic disease, where the valvular lesion is discovered incidentally
- Congestive cardiac failure triggered by increased cardiac demand, such as in pregnancy or anaemia

**Differential diagnosis**

- Other causes of cardiac failure

**Investigations**

- Chest X-ray
- ECG, where available
- Echocardiography

**Management**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Treat heart failure if present. | HC4 |
| Provide prophylaxis for life as described under rheumatic fever. | NR |
| Consider cardiac surgery if necessary. This is available only at national referral hospital level. | NR |

**Prevention**

- Prevent recurrent rheumatic fever through continuous prophylaxis.
- Diagnose and treat streptococcal throat infection early.
- Strengthen adherence to long-term prophylaxis in patients with previous rheumatic fever.

### 4.1.11 Stroke

**ICD-10 CODE:** I63

Stroke is a cerebral neurological dysfunction caused by a problem in blood circulation to the brain. It may result from a clot, causing ischaemic stroke, or bleeding, causing haemorrhagic stroke.

**Causes**

- Clot, either a thrombus in a brain vessel or an embolus from a clot elsewhere. This is the most common cause.
- Haemorrhage, either spontaneous or due to trauma.

**Clinical features**

- Focal neurological deficits, such as one-sided weakness of the face, arm, or leg
- Hemiparesis or hemiplegia
- Difficulty in speaking
- Difficulty in swallowing
- Severe headache, especially in haemorrhagic stroke
- Altered level of consciousness
- Convulsions

**Investigations**

- CT scan of the brain

In the absence of neuroimaging, the following clinical features may help distinguish stroke subtypes:

| Type | Clinical course | Risk factors | Other clues |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intracerebral haemorrhage | Gradual progression over minutes or hours | Hypertension, trauma, bleeding disorders, illicit drug use | Patients may have reduced alertness and severe headache. |
| Subarachnoid haemorrhage | Abrupt onset of very severe headache. Focal symptoms are less common. | Smoking, hypertension, illicit drug use, or sometimes no clear risk factor due to rupture of congenital aneurysms | Patients may have reduced alertness. It may occur in young people. |
| Ischaemic stroke, thrombotic | Gradual development of focal deficits over hours or days | Age, smoking, diabetes, dyslipidaemia | Symptoms may improve and worsen in the following days. |
| Ischaemic stroke, embolic | Sudden onset of focal deficits | Age, smoking, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, valvular heart disease, and arrhythmias | Often improves slowly. |

**Management**

**General care**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Ensure airway and respiration if the patient is unconscious. | H |
| Do not give anything by mouth before assessing swallowing ability, to avoid aspiration. | H |
| Provide IV fluids or nasogastric tube feeding for hydration and nutrition if the patient is unable to swallow. | H |
| Control blood sugar with insulin if the patient is diabetic. | H |

**Management of ischaemic stroke**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Give aspirin 150–300 mg every 24 hours. | H |
| In the acute phase, treat hypertension only if it is extreme, above 220/120 mmHg, or if there are other complications such as pulmonary oedema or angina. | H |
| If there are no acute complications, restart antihypertensive treatment 24 hours after the event and reduce blood pressure slowly. | H |
| Consider DVT prophylaxis with enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily. | H |

**Management of clinically haemorrhagic stroke**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Provide supportive care as above. | H |
| Refer for CT scan and neurosurgical evaluation. | H |

**Chronic care of ischaemic stroke**

| Treatment | LOC |
|---|---|
| Start early mobilisation and physiotherapy. | H |
| Give aspirin 75–100 mg once daily for life. | H |
| Give atorvastatin 40 mg once daily for life. | H |
| Control risk factors. | H |
